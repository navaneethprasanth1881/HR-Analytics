# 📊 HR Analytics – Employee Attrition Analysis

## 📌 Project Overview

Employee attrition is a major challenge for organizations as it leads to the loss of skilled employees, institutional knowledge, increased recruitment expenses, and reduced operational efficiency.

This project focuses on analyzing employee data from **SimpleYard**, a fast-growing organization, to identify patterns and factors associated with employee attrition.

Using Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA), the project investigates how factors such as salary, employee satisfaction, department, number of projects, working hours, years spent at the company, and promotions relate to employee turnover.

The primary objective is to help organizations understand employee attrition patterns and identify areas where HR strategies can be improved.

## 🎯 Objectives

- Analyze overall employee attrition rates.
- Identify departments with higher employee turnover.
- Investigate the relationship between salary and employee attrition.
- Understand how employee satisfaction influences attrition.
- Analyze the impact of project allocation and workload.
- Explore the relationship between employee tenure and project involvement.
- Investigate the role of promotions in employee retention.
- Identify important factors associated with employee turnover.

## 📂 Dataset Overview

The dataset contains information about **14,999 employees**.

| Employee Status | Number of Employees |
|---|---:|
| Employees who stayed | 11,482 |
| Employees who left | 3,517 |
| Total employees | 14,999 |

**Overall Attrition Rate:** Approximately 23.45%

The analysis considers the following employee-related attributes:

- Department
- Salary level
- Satisfaction level
- Last evaluation
- Number of projects
- Average monthly working hours
- Time spent at the company
- Work accidents
- Promotions in the last five years
- Employee attrition status

## 🔍 Key Insights & Findings

### 1. Employee Attrition by Department

- The Sales department accounts for the highest number of employees who left.
- However, the HR department has the highest attrition rate, followed by Accounting and other departments.
- This indicates that employee turnover varies across departments.

### 2. Project Allocation and Attrition

- Employees with fewer than three projects show a notable number of departures.
- However, the number of projects alone does not directly explain employee attrition.
- Other employee-related factors should also be considered.

### 3. Relationship Between Projects and Tenure

- Number of projects and time spent at the company show a weak positive correlation.
- Employees who spend more time in the organization tend to receive more projects, although this relationship is not consistent.

### 4. Salary and Employee Attrition

- Employees belonging to the low-salary category account for the highest number of departures.
- Medium-salary employees also contribute significantly to attrition.
- High-salary employees show considerably fewer departures.
- Salary appears to be an important factor associated with employee retention.

### 5. Employee Satisfaction Level

- Employees who leave the organization generally have lower satisfaction levels.
- The analysis indicates a negative relationship between employee satisfaction and attrition.
- Improving employee satisfaction may help organizations improve retention.

### 6. Working Hours and Project Allocation

- Average monthly working hours and the number of projects have a positive correlation.
- Employees assigned more projects generally tend to work more monthly hours.

### 7. Employee Promotions

- Only 19 employees received promotions in the last five years, according to the analysis.
- Limited promotion opportunities may be associated with employee dissatisfaction and attrition.

### 8. Work Accidents

- Work accidents do not appear to have a strong direct relationship with employee attrition.
- The analysis identified 169 employees who left despite having experienced work accidents.

## 📈 Exploratory Data Analysis

The project includes the following visualizations:

- Department-wise employee attrition analysis.
- Project allocation versus employee departures.
- Correlation heatmap of numerical employee attributes.
- Salary-wise employee attrition analysis.
- Employee satisfaction comparison using box plots.
- Relationship between average monthly hours and number of projects.

These visualizations help identify patterns, relationships, and potential factors associated with employee turnover.

## 📝 Conclusion

The Exploratory Data Analysis reveals that employee attrition at SimpleYard is associated with several organizational and employee-related factors.

The major factors identified include:

1. **Employee Satisfaction:** Lower satisfaction levels are associated with higher attrition.
2. **Salary:** Employees in lower salary categories show higher attrition.
3. **Project Allocation:** Employees with fewer than three projects account for a notable number of departures.
4. **Promotions:** Limited promotion opportunities may contribute to employee turnover.
5. **Department:** Attrition patterns vary across departments, with HR having the highest attrition rate.

Overall, the analysis highlights the importance of employee satisfaction, competitive compensation, appropriate workload distribution, and career development opportunities in improving employee retention.
